3 Data

3.1 Data Source

The data sets used were sourced from the United States Department of Agriculture’s (USDA’s) Food Access Research Atlas for 2015 and 2019. The Food Access Research Atlas Data contains food access flags for United States census tracts using different measurement thresholds, combined with demographic and socioeconomic data from multiple data sources. The USDA defines being low access as being geographically far from a “food store”, e.g., a supermarket, supercenter, or grocery store. Both data sets use a combination of two lists of food stores to represent sources of affordable and nutritious food from 2015 and 2019, respectively.

Data used in the 2015 Atlas includes population data from the 2010 Census of the Population, urban designation provided by the 2010 Census at the block level, and data on income, vehicle availability, and SNAP participation from the 2010-14 American Community Survey (ACS). Data from the 2019 Atlas uses the same population data as 2015, with urban designations from the 2019 urbanized area geographies, and data on income, vehicle availability, and SNAP participation from the 2014-18 ACS. Both data sets are based on 2010 census-tract boundaries and contain the same classification definitions, allowing us to combine data sets into a single panel data.

3.2 Unit of Observation

The unit of observation for both data sets is U.S. census tracts, with each row representing one census tract. Census tracts are small, geographic subdivisions of state counties.

3.3 Key Variables

The outcome variables used are LAhalfand10 and LA1and10, which are low access flags at different measurement thresholds. A tract is deemed “low access” if at least 500 people or at least 33% of the population is greater than the measurement thresholds miles away from the nearest food store. LAhalfand10 is measured by being a half mile away for urban tracts and 10 miles for nonurban tracts, while LA1and10 is measured by being 1 mile away for urban tracts and 10 miles away for nonurban tracts.

This research focuses on socioeconomic variables, including low income tracts, poverty rate, accessibility to a personal vehicle, and population’s access to government assisted food program, SNAP. Demographic variables related to ethnicity and age are kept as control variables, while census tract and year variables will be used as fixed effects to control for time-invariant within-county characteristics and year shocks.

3.4 Data Cleaning

The 2015 and 2019 data sets were prepared for combining into the main panel data by converting variables into their proper types and binding the rows together, filtering to keep data from New York state. Demographic data that is represented as raw counts, like TractSNAP, which is the total count of housing units receiving SNAP benefits, were recalculated as the total share of population for fair comparison across tracts. Variables with a high missing value count were dropped completely, and observations with missing values were dropped for consistency and in order to run lasso regression for variable selection later on.
